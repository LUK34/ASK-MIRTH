# HL7 PID, PV1, GT1, and IN1 Segments — Part 2

> Beginner-friendly study notes and an implementation reference prepared from **2.PID_PV1_GT_IN1 Part 2.mp4**.

## Learning objectives

After studying this lesson, you should be able to:

- understand the remaining PID fields, PID-25 through PID-40;
- explain the purpose of the PV1 patient-visit segment;
- identify patient class, location, providers, service, visit number, and financial class in PV1;
- explain the purpose of the GT1 guarantor segment;
- distinguish a patient, guarantor, insured person, and insurance company;
- recognize the main IN1 health-plan and insurance fields shown in the lesson;
- understand how PID, PV1, GT1, and IN1 work together in an ADT message;
- extract important fields in Mirth Connect without confusing patient, visit, guarantor, and insurance identifiers.

## 1. Lesson scope

The video continues the PID field table from Part 1 and then introduces three additional segments:

| Segment | Standard name | Information carried |
|---|---|---|
| `PID` | Patient Identification | Patient identity and demographics |
| `PV1` | Patient Visit | Visit, encounter, location, provider, service, and financial information |
| `GT1` | Guarantor | Person or organization financially responsible for the account |
| `IN1` | Insurance | Health plan, insurance company, coverage, and insured-person information |

A simplified message may look like:

```hl7
MSH|^~\&|SOURCE_APP|SOURCE_FACILITY|TARGET_APP|TARGET_FACILITY|20260922120000+0300||ADT^A04^ADT_A01|MSG000001|T|2.5.1
EVN|A04|20260922120000+0300
PID|1||100001^^^KNG^MR||TEST^PATIENT^ONE||19900101|M
PV1|1|O|CLINIC^ROOM1^BED1||||731^TEST^DOCTOR||||MED|||||||VN000001
GT1|1|G000001|TEST^GUARANTOR^ONE|||55500000||19800101|M|SELF
IN1|1|PLAN01|INS001|TEST INSURANCE COMPANY||||GROUP01||||20260101|20261231||TEST^INSURED^ONE|SELF|19900101
```

All data in this README is fictional and intended only for training.

## 2. How the segments relate

The four segments describe different business concepts:

| Question | Segment/field area |
|---|---|
| Who is the patient? | PID |
| What visit or encounter is this? | PV1 |
| Who is financially responsible? | GT1 |
| Which plan/company provides insurance coverage? | IN1 |
| Who holds the insurance policy? | IN1 insured-person fields |

These people or organizations can be different. The patient is not automatically the guarantor, and the guarantor is not automatically the insured person.

### PID-24 versus PID-25

These fields answer different questions:

- `PID-24` — Was the patient part of a multiple birth?
- `PID-25` — What was the patient's birth order?

Example:

```text
PID-24 = Y
PID-25 = 2
```

This could indicate the second child in a multiple birth.

### PID-29 and PID-30 must agree

If `PID-30` says the patient is deceased, `PID-29` may contain the death date/time when known. Receivers should define how to handle contradictory combinations such as:

- death indicator `N` with a populated death timestamp;
- death indicator `Y` with no timestamp;
- a death timestamp earlier than the date of birth.

Do not automatically mark a patient deceased based on incomplete or unverified data.

### PID-31 and PID-32

`PID-31` indicates that identity is unknown. `PID-32` can describe the reliability of identity information. These fields can be important for emergency or unidentified-patient workflows.

Never merge an unidentified patient into an existing record solely because a name or date of birth appears similar.

## 6. Important PV1 concepts

### PV1-2 — Patient Class

Patient class classifies the encounter, not the patient's identity.

Common values vary by version and profile. Frequently encountered examples include:

| Example | Typical meaning |
|---|---|
| `I` | Inpatient |
| `O` | Outpatient |
| `E` | Emergency |
| `P` | Preadmit |

Always use the value set agreed with the receiving system.

### PV1-3 — Assigned Patient Location

PV1-3 uses the PL datatype. A simplified location may contain:

```text
Point of Care ^ Room ^ Bed ^ Facility
```

Example:

```text
CLINIC^ROOM1^BED1^KNG_HQ
```

Carets separate location components. They do not create additional PV1 fields.

### PV1-7, PV1-8, and PV1-17

These are different provider roles:

- `PV1-7` — attending doctor;
- `PV1-8` — referring doctor;
- `PV1-17` — admitting doctor.

One provider may fill multiple roles, but the fields should not be treated as interchangeable.

### PV1-19 — Visit Number

PV1-19 identifies the encounter or visit. It is not the same as:

- PID-3 patient identifier;
- PID-18 patient account number;
- MSH-10 message control ID;
- IN1 policy or insurance-company identifier.

| Identifier | Identifies |
|---|---|
| `MSH-10` | HL7 message |
| `PID-3` | Patient |
| `PID-18` | Patient account |
| `PV1-19` | Visit/encounter |

## 7. GT1 — Guarantor Segment

The lesson defines GT1 as the segment carrying the patient's guarantor information.

A guarantor is the person or organization financially responsible for the account. The guarantor may be:

- the patient;
- a parent or guardian;
- a spouse;
- an employer;
- another person;
- an organization.

```hl7
GT1|1|G000001|TEST^GUARANTOR^ONE||TEST ADDRESS|55500000||19800101|M|SELF
```

### Repeating GT1 segments

An account can have more than one guarantor. GT1-1 identifies each GT1 occurrence:

```hl7
GT1|1|G000001|TEST^PRIMARY^GUARANTOR|...
GT1|2|G000002|TEST^SECONDARY^GUARANTOR|...
```

Do not confuse multiple GT1 segments with repetitions inside a single GT1 field.

## 9. IN1 — Insurance Segment

## 11. Patient, guarantor, and insured person

These roles must remain separate:

| Role | Where represented | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Patient | PID | Child receiving treatment |
| Guarantor | GT1 | Parent responsible for payment |
| Insured person | IN1-16 onward | Parent who holds the insurance policy |
| Insurance company | IN1-3 through IN1-7 | Organization providing coverage |

The same person can occupy more than one role, but the message must still place each role's information in its proper segment and field.

## 12. Coverage dates

`IN1-12` and `IN1-13` define the plan's effective period:

```text
IN1-12 = 20260101
IN1-13 = 20261231
```

Before treating coverage as active, validate:

- effective date is not after the expiration date;
- the relevant service date falls within the coverage period;
- the date precision is sufficient;
- an empty expiration date is interpreted according to the payer agreement, not automatically as invalid.

Coverage validation usually requires payer/business rules beyond the presence of IN1.

## 14. Mirth Connect extraction examples

```javascript
// Patient
var patientId = msg['PID']['PID.3']['PID.3.1'].toString();
var patientAccount = msg['PID']['PID.18']['PID.18.1'].toString();

// Visit
var patientClass = msg['PV1']['PV1.2']['PV1.2.1'].toString();
var pointOfCare = msg['PV1']['PV1.3']['PV1.3.1'].toString();
var attendingDoctorId = msg['PV1']['PV1.7']['PV1.7.1'].toString();
var visitNumber = msg['PV1']['PV1.19']['PV1.19.1'].toString();

// Guarantor
var guarantorNumber = msg['GT1']['GT1.2']['GT1.2.1'].toString();
var guarantorFamilyName = msg['GT1']['GT1.3']['GT1.3.1'].toString();
var guarantorRelationship = msg['GT1']['GT1.11']['GT1.11.1'].toString();

// Insurance
var healthPlanId = msg['IN1']['IN1.2']['IN1.2.1'].toString();
var insuranceCompanyId = msg['IN1']['IN1.3']['IN1.3.1'].toString();
var insuranceCompanyName = msg['IN1']['IN1.4']['IN1.4.1'].toString();
var groupNumber = msg['IN1']['IN1.8']['IN1.8.1'].toString();
var insuredRelationship = msg['IN1']['IN1.17']['IN1.17.1'].toString();
```

These snippets illustrate common access patterns. Repeating segments and fields require deliberate iteration rather than assuming only one value exists.

### Basic cross-segment validation

```javascript
var patientId = msg['PID']['PID.3']['PID.3.1'].toString();
var patientClass = msg['PV1']['PV1.2']['PV1.2.1'].toString();
var visitNumber = msg['PV1']['PV1.19']['PV1.19.1'].toString();

if (!patientId) {
    throw 'PID-3 Patient Identifier List is required';
}

if (!patientClass) {
    throw 'PV1-2 Patient Class is required for this interface';
}

if (!visitNumber) {
    throw 'PV1-19 Visit Number is required for this workflow';
}
```

Required fields must come from the agreed profile; do not impose one workflow's requirements on every HL7 message type.

## 15. Validation checklist

### PID

- [ ] Select PID-3 using the agreed assigning authority and identifier type.
- [ ] Validate birth and death information for contradictions.
- [ ] Treat unidentified-patient records with additional safeguards.
- [ ] Confirm demographic update time/facility when used.

### PV1

- [ ] Validate patient class against the correct value set.
- [ ] Parse assigned location as PL components.
- [ ] Keep attending, referring, consulting, and admitting provider roles separate.
- [ ] Treat PV1-19 as a visit identifier, not a patient identifier.
- [ ] Validate financial-class codes against the profile.

### GT1

- [ ] Keep guarantor data separate from patient data.
- [ ] Support multiple GT1 segments when permitted.
- [ ] Validate relationship and guarantor-type codes.
- [ ] Protect financial, income, and legacy identifier data.
- [ ] Check death flag/date consistency if supplied.

### IN1

- [ ] Validate the health-plan and insurance-company identifiers.
- [ ] Keep company, group, and insured-person details separate.
- [ ] Validate coverage dates against the relevant service date.
- [ ] Validate insured relationship using the agreed table.
- [ ] Support multiple IN1 segments for multiple coverages when permitted.
- [ ] Do not consider coverage verified merely because an IN1 segment exists.

## 16. Privacy and security considerations

These segments can contain highly sensitive information:

- PID — personal identifiers and demographics;
- PV1 — visit, location, and provider details;
- GT1 — addresses, financial responsibility, employment, income, and legacy identifiers;
- IN1 — insurance membership, coverage, employer, and insured-person information.

Production guidance:

- avoid logging complete segments;
- mask identifiers in support logs;
- restrict access to billing and guarantor data;
- encrypt messages in transit according to organizational policy;
- define log retention and deletion rules;
- ensure test environments use fictional or properly de-identified data.

## 17. Common mistakes

1. **Using PID for visit information.** Visit details belong in PV1.
2. **Using PV1-19 as the patient ID.** It identifies the visit, not the person.
3. **Treating patient, guarantor, and insured person as automatically identical.** They are separate roles.
4. **Flattening PL, XCN, CX, XPN, XAD, or XTN into arbitrary strings.** Their components carry meaning.
5. **Ignoring repeated providers or identifiers.** The required value may not be the first repetition.
6. **Assuming GT1 appears only once.** Multiple guarantors may be represented.
7. **Assuming IN1 appears only once.** Multiple coverages may be present.
8. **Treating an IN1 segment as proof of active coverage.** Dates and payer rules must be checked.
9. **Using insurance-company ID as the policy or member ID.** These identifiers have different meanings.
10. **Logging complete PID, GT1, or IN1 segments.** This can expose personal and financial information.
11. **Ignoring the HL7 version.** Field status, datatype, and allowed values can change.
12. **Removing empty fields.** This shifts later values into incorrect positions.
