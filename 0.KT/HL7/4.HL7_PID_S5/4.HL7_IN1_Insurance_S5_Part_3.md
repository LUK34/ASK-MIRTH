# HL7 IN1 Insurance Segment — Part 3

> Beginner-friendly study notes and an implementation reference prepared from **2.PID_PV1_GT_IN1 Part 3.mp4**.

## Learning objectives

After studying this lesson, you should be able to:

- explain the purpose of the IN1 segment;
- distinguish the insurance company, health plan, group, subscriber, insured person, and beneficiary;
- identify the IN1 fields displayed from `IN1-1` through `IN1-54`;
- understand assignment and coordination of benefits;
- interpret notice, eligibility, verification, policy-limit, coverage-type, and signature fields;
- use HL7 relationship table 0063 for `IN1-17`;
- relate IN1 insurance data to PID, PV1, GT1, and IN2;
- extract and validate insurance information safely in Mirth Connect.

## 1. Lesson focus

The video continues the IN1 definition table and reviews a complete ADT message containing:

```text
MSH  Message Header
EVN  Event Information
PID  Patient Identification
PV1  Patient Visit
PV2  Additional Visit Information
GT1  Guarantor
IN1  Insurance

```

The core lesson is how IN1 represents insurance coverage and the relationship between the person holding the insurance and the patient benefiting from it.

## 2. What is the IN1 segment?

**IN1** is the Insurance segment. It identifies the health plan, insurance company, group, policy dates, insured person, relationship to the patient, coverage details, and related administrative information.

Simplified example:

```hl7
IN1|1|PLAN01|INS001|TEST INSURANCE COMPANY|TEST INSURANCE ADDRESS|||GROUP01|TEST GROUP|||20260101|20261231||TEST^SUBSCRIBER^ONE|SELF|19800101
```

An ADT message can contain multiple IN1 segments when a patient has more than one coverage.

```hl7
IN1|1|PRIMARY_PLAN|PRIMARY_PAYER|PRIMARY INSURANCE|...
IN1|2|SECONDARY_PLAN|SECONDARY_PAYER|SECONDARY INSURANCE|...
```

`IN1-1` identifies the order of the IN1 occurrence. It does not by itself replace an explicit coordination-of-benefits priority.

## 3. Insurance terminology

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Insurance company/payer | Organization providing or administering coverage |
| Health plan | Specific coverage product or plan |
| Group | Employer or membership group under which coverage is organized |
| Subscriber/policy holder | Person who holds the insurance contract |
| Insured person | Person represented by the insurance record; often the subscriber in IN1 usage |
| Beneficiary/dependent | Person receiving benefits through the subscriber's coverage |
| Patient | Person receiving healthcare services |
| Guarantor | Person or organization financially responsible for the account |

The video summarizes the distinction as:

```text
Holds the insurance  -> subscriber
Benefits from it     -> beneficiary
```

These roles can overlap, but they must not be assumed to be the same.

### Example family scenario

- A parent obtains an insurance policy through an employer.
- The parent is the subscriber.
- A child receives treatment using that policy.
- The child is the patient and beneficiary/dependent.
- The parent may also be the guarantor.

In this situation:

- PID describes the child;
- GT1 can describe the financially responsible parent;
- IN1 describes the plan and subscriber relationship;
- `IN1-17` indicates how the insured/subscriber relates to the patient.

### Assignment of benefits

Assignment of benefits generally indicates whether payment rights have been assigned to the healthcare provider. The exact codes and legal meaning depend on the payer contract and jurisdiction.

Do not interpret a non-empty `IN1-20` as guaranteed payment.

### Coordination of benefits

When multiple IN1 segments exist, coordination-of-benefits information helps determine which coverage is primary, secondary, or otherwise ordered.

```hl7
IN1|1|PRIMARY_PLAN|...|||||||||||||||||...|PRIMARY
IN1|2|SECONDARY_PLAN|...|||||||||||||||||...|SECONDARY
```

The example is conceptual. Use the exact fields and permitted codes defined by the receiving profile.

### Eligibility versus coverage

`IN1-25` and `IN1-26` report eligibility-related information, but the presence of these fields is not permanent proof of coverage. Eligibility can change after verification.

Systems should retain:

- when verification occurred;
- who or what verified it;
- the payer response/reference when available;
- the service date to which verification applied.

### Withdrawn fields

The selected definition marks `IN1-38`, `IN1-40`, and `IN1-41` as withdrawn.

For new integrations:

- do not use withdrawn fields unless a legacy interface explicitly requires them;
- document the compatibility requirement;
- avoid moving another value into a withdrawn field's position;
- preserve empty delimiters so subsequent fields remain correctly positioned.

### Policy deductible and policy limits

A deductible is not the same as the policy limit:

- deductible — amount the insured may need to pay before coverage applies according to plan rules;
- policy limit — maximum covered amount or service duration under specified conditions.

Financial interpretation requires payer rules; an HL7 field alone is insufficient for final billing decisions.

### SELF scenario

If the patient holds the policy personally:

```text
IN1-16 = patient/subscriber name
IN1-17 = SELF
```

The exact SELF code may be defined by the profile or local table. Do not assume a display word is the permitted code.

### Dependent scenario

If a parent is insured and the child is the patient:

```text
PID-5  = child's name
IN1-16 = parent's name
IN1-17 = child/dependent relationship code
```

Relationship direction matters: `IN1-17` describes the insured person's relationship to the patient according to the field definition/profile. Confirm local usage because implementations sometimes interpret the direction inconsistently.

## 8. IN1-47 — Coverage Type

`IN1-47` uses a coded value associated with table 0309 in the displayed definition.

Coverage type can describe the category of coverage. Exact values are implementation-dependent. Do not invent a code from a display description or reuse a plan-type code from `IN1-15` without agreement.

| Field | Question answered |
|---|---|
| `IN1-15` Plan Type | What kind of plan is this? |
| `IN1-47` Coverage Type | What category/type of coverage applies? |

The distinction may be narrowed or customized by the local profile.

## 9. IN1-49 and PID-3 are different

`PID-3` identifies the patient. `IN1-49` identifies the insured/subscriber in the insurance context.

They may contain the same person in a self-covered scenario, but they are different data concepts.

| Field | Identifier belongs to |
|---|---|
| `PID-3` | Patient |
| `PV1-19` | Visit/encounter |
| `GT1-2` | Guarantor |
| `IN1-3` | Insurance company |
| `IN1-36` | Insurance policy |
| `IN1-49` | Insured/subscriber |

Never join or merge records using identifier text alone. Include assigning authority and identifier type where available.

The recording contains a longer dummy message. This version is normalized and shortened for teaching.

### Segment interpretation

1. MSH identifies PM as sender and EHR as receiver.
2. EVN identifies the A28 event.
3. PID identifies the patient and contains repeated identifiers.
4. PV1 describes the patient visit.
5. GT1 identifies the guarantor.
6. IN1 represents self-pay/insurance information and the insured relationship.
7. IN2 provides additional insurance details.

## 11. Repetition reminder from PID-3

The video revisits three PID-3 repetitions:

```text
PID-3 repetition 1 = 9541236254
PID-3 repetition 2 = 81698786^^^MRN
PID-3 repetition 3 = 0008169^^^SYSID
```

Combined form:

```hl7
9541236254~81698786^^^MRN~0008169^^^SYSID
```

- `~` separates repeated identifiers.
- `^` separates components inside each identifier.

This matters when comparing PID-3 patient identifiers with insurance identifiers such as IN1-3 or IN1-49.

## 12. Mirth Connect extraction examples

```javascript
var setId = msg['IN1']['IN1.1']['IN1.1.1'].toString();
var healthPlanId = msg['IN1']['IN1.2']['IN1.2.1'].toString();
var payerId = msg['IN1']['IN1.3']['IN1.3.1'].toString();
var payerName = msg['IN1']['IN1.4']['IN1.4.1'].toString();
var groupNumber = msg['IN1']['IN1.8']['IN1.8.1'].toString();
var effectiveDate = msg['IN1']['IN1.12']['IN1.12.1'].toString();
var expirationDate = msg['IN1']['IN1.13']['IN1.13.1'].toString();
var insuredFamilyName = msg['IN1']['IN1.16']['IN1.16.1'].toString();
var insuredGivenName = msg['IN1']['IN1.16']['IN1.16.2'].toString();
var relationship = msg['IN1']['IN1.17']['IN1.17.1'].toString();
var policyNumber = msg['IN1']['IN1.36']['IN1.36.1'].toString();
var coverageType = msg['IN1']['IN1.47']['IN1.47.1'].toString();
var insuredId = msg['IN1']['IN1.49']['IN1.49.1'].toString();
```

### Coverage-date validation

```javascript
var effectiveDate = msg['IN1']['IN1.12']['IN1.12.1'].toString();
var expirationDate = msg['IN1']['IN1.13']['IN1.13.1'].toString();

if (effectiveDate && expirationDate && effectiveDate > expirationDate) {
    throw 'IN1 coverage dates are invalid: effective date is after expiration date';
}
```

Lexical comparison is safe only when both values use the same complete `YYYYMMDD` format. For partial dates or timestamps, use a proper HL7 date parser.

### Multiple IN1 segments

```javascript
var coverageCount = 0;

for each (var in1 in msg['IN1']) {
    coverageCount++;

    var sequence = in1['IN1.1']['IN1.1.1'].toString();
    var planId = in1['IN1.2']['IN1.2.1'].toString();
    var payerId = in1['IN1.3']['IN1.3.1'].toString();

    logger.info('Coverage sequence=' + sequence
        + ', plan=' + planId
        + ', payer=' + payerId);
}
```

Avoid logging identifiers or insurance details in production unless they are appropriately masked and access-controlled.

## 13. Insurance validation checklist

- [ ] At least one plan/payer identifier is available when insurance is required.
- [ ] IN1-1 sequence values are present and non-conflicting.
- [ ] Health-plan ID and insurance-company ID are not treated as interchangeable.
- [ ] Group number/name belong to the correct payer and plan.
- [ ] Effective date is not after expiration date.
- [ ] The service date falls within the relevant coverage period when required.
- [ ] IN1-16 represents the insured/subscriber, not automatically the patient.
- [ ] IN1-17 uses the agreed relationship code and direction.
- [ ] Coordination-of-benefits priority is consistent across multiple coverages.
- [ ] Eligibility and verification timestamps are current enough for the workflow.
- [ ] The verifier and source/reference are retained when required.
- [ ] Withdrawn fields are not used for new mappings without a compatibility need.
- [ ] Policy, patient, guarantor, visit, payer, and subscriber identifiers remain distinct.
- [ ] Repeating CX/XPN/XAD/XTN fields are processed deliberately.
- [ ] Insurance data is masked in logs and protected in transit and at rest.

## 14. Common mistakes

1. **Assuming patient means subscriber.** A dependent patient may use another person's policy.
2. **Assuming guarantor means subscriber.** Financial responsibility and policy ownership are separate roles.
3. **Reversing the direction of IN1-17.** Confirm whose relationship to whom the profile expects.
4. **Treating plan ID and payer ID as the same value.** IN1-2 and IN1-3 identify different concepts.
5. **Using IN1-1 as the only coverage priority.** Coordination-of-benefits fields and payer rules also matter.
6. **Assuming an IN1 segment proves active coverage.** Coverage and eligibility must be verified.
7. **Ignoring IN1-29 and IN1-30.** Verification time and verifier are important audit information.
8. **Using withdrawn room-rate or limit fields in a new interface.** Follow the declared version/profile.
9. **Comparing policy dates as arbitrary strings.** Respect HL7 precision and format.
10. **Using PID-3 as the insurance member ID without agreement.** Patient and subscriber identifiers can differ.
11. **Reading only the first IN1 segment.** Secondary or additional coverage may be present.
12. **Logging full insurance segments.** IN1 contains personal, employment, financial, and policy data.
